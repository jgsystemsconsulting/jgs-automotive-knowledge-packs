# Glossary: nhtsa-vehicle

Terms as the pinned NHTSA sources and the chapters use them, with the chapters that carry the detail. Industry standards named beside the guidance appear as designations only; their text lives with the owners, routed through the signpost packs.

- **[G.x] / [T.x] practice labels**: S1's enumeration scheme. The general block holds 45 practices printed under 44 distinct labels: there is no standalone [G.1], and [G.18] is reused for two different practices (the section 4 NIST CSF practice and the 4.2.9 attack-intelligence practice). ch02 carries [G.2]-[G.24], ch03 carries [G.25]-[G.45]; the 25 technical practices [T.1]-[T.25] live in ch04. → ch02, ch03, ch04
- **ADS (Automated Driving System)**: The combined hardware and software that performs part or all of the driving task on a sustained basis. ADS 2.0's voluntary guidance targets SAE Levels 3-5, where the system, not a human driver, performs the driving-related tasks while engaged. → ch01, ch05, ch06
- **ASIL (Automotive Safety Integrity Level)**: The risk classification label used inside ISO 26262 programmes (commonly QM and A through D) for the integrity demand of a hazardous event after severity, exposure, and controllability are considered. It is not an FMVSS grade and not an SAE automation level; the standard is citation-only here. → ch08
- **Auto-ISAC (Automotive Information Sharing and Analysis Center)**: The sector's sharing body, formed under the EO 13691 private-sector sharing policy. Extended industry members join, share timely vulnerability and attack intelligence, and collaborate on containment even when their own products are not yet hit. → ch02, ch03, ch05
- **CBI (confidential business information)**: Material a published Voluntary Safety Self-Assessment must not contain. → ch05
- **CISA / US-CERT**: The federal incident notification channel: S1 points vehicle cybersecurity incidents to CISA/US-CERT under the US-CERT Federal Incident Notification Guidelines. → ch02, ch03
- **CMV (commercial motor vehicle)**: Interstate CMV driver and carrier operations sit with FMCSA, outside ADS 2.0's guidance scope unless a separate FMCSA waiver or exemption path applies. → ch05
- **Coordinated vulnerability disclosure**: The published reporting policy and mechanism S1 expects each organization to run (accessible channel, acknowledgment, triage, remediation tracked to closure) and ADS 2.0's cybersecurity element names alongside incident response plans. → ch03, ch05
- **COTS (commercial off-the-shelf)**: Off-the-shelf or open-source ECU software that S1 says to evaluate against known vulnerabilities before use, with a written vulnerability analysis for each finding. → ch02
- **CSMS (cyber security management system)**: The management-system regulation concept behind UNECE R155, named beside S1's corporate process expectations. The regulation is free to download but (c) UNECE, so it is citation-only; automotive-signpost holds the row. → ch08
- **ECU (electronic control unit)**: The vehicle computer whose operational hardware and software must be inventoried in detail ([G.10]-[G.11]) and whose debug, diagnostic, network, and update surfaces the [T.x] controls protect. → ch02, ch04
- **ESC (electronic stability control)**: Computer-controlled yaw and individual-wheel brake torque modulation required as equipment and performance by FMVSS 126 (light vehicles) and 136 (heavy vehicles), with required malfunction telltale behavior. → ch07
- **FHWA (Federal Highway Administration)**: The DOT lane for infrastructure safety, evaluation, planning, and maintenance; the partner for MUTCD alignment as machine vision raises the cost of low-quality markings and signs. → ch06
- **FMCSA (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration)**: Owns safe operation of interstate motor carriers and commercial drivers, plus registration and insurance requirements in that domain. → ch05, ch06
- **FMVSS (Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards)**: The binding US vehicle performance and equipment standards codified at 49 CFR Part 571. Manufacturers self-certify each vehicle to applicable standards at sale; NHTSA enforces through compliance testing, defects investigation, and recall. → ch01, ch07
- **Functional safety**: The engineering discipline addressing unreasonable risk from hazards caused by malfunctioning behaviour of electrical/electronic/programmable electronic systems. IEC 61508 is the generic baseline; ISO 26262 is the road-vehicle derivation. Both are named, not reproduced, here. → ch08
- **GVWR (gross vehicle weight rating)**: The weight cutoff in Subpart A definitions and standard application paragraphs that decides which standards bite (for example the 4,536 kg / 10,000 lb band in FMVSS 126 and 138). → ch07
- **HMI (human machine interface)**: ADS safety design element 6: indicators of proper function, engagement, unavailability, malfunction, and control-transfer requests for drivers, occupants, and external actors, plus accessibility and remote-dispatcher support. On the regulation side, FMVSS 101 governs the controls, telltales, and indicators vocabulary. → ch05, ch07
- **IBR (incorporation by reference)**: The Part 571 Subpart A machinery that pulls external test methods, drawings, and industry publications (SAE, ASTM, and similar) into the standards. Compliance follows the incorporated editions; this pack does not reproduce IBR texts. → ch07
- **Layered cybersecurity (compromise-assumed)**: S1's design posture: assume some vehicle systems can be reached, reduce the chance a compromise escalates, and limit the damage. Unreasonable safety-critical risk is removed or mitigated by design; residual function sits behind risk-appropriate layers with cybersecurity expectations pushed to suppliers ([G.7]-[G.9]). → ch02
- **MRC (minimal risk condition)**: The condition the vehicle moves toward when a cyber event is detected ([G.17]) or when the ADS detects malfunction, degradation, or ODD exit (element 4). It varies by failure, often a controlled stop, and is reachable without driver intervention at higher automation. An operational safety outcome idea, not an integrity level. → ch02, ch05
- **MUTCD (Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices)**: The infrastructure standard States should keep markings and traffic-control devices aligned with, as ADS machine perception replaces forgiving human vision. → ch06
- **NIST CSF (Cybersecurity Framework)**: The industry scaffold S1's opening general practice anchors on: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover, prioritizing safety-critical controls. → ch02
- **NIST FIPS 140 series**: The publication series S1 names for cryptographic technique selection and currency; named only, no text. → ch04
- **ODD (operational design domain)**: ADS safety design element 2: the documented boundary of roadway types, geography, speed range, environment, and other constraints where an ADS is intended to function. ODD exit or dynamic loss should drive transition toward a minimal risk condition. → ch05
- **OEDR (object and event detection and response)**: ADS safety design element 3: the detection of driving-task-relevant circumstances (other road users, emergency vehicles, work zones, manual traffic control) and the appropriate response, performed by the ADS while engaged inside its ODD. → ch05
- **OTA (over-the-air) updates**: Remote software updates whose package, servers, transmission path, and process must all hold integrity, with explicit threat models for compromised servers, insiders, man-in-the-middle attacks, and protocol vulnerabilities ([T.24]-[T.25]). → ch04
- **Part 571**: The 49 CFR part codifying the FMVSS: Subpart A carries the general machinery (definitions, IBR, applicability, dates, separability, seating designation); Subpart B carries the numbered standards. Binding through self-certification. → ch01, ch07
- **S1 / S2 / S3 (source labels)**: This pack's labels for the pinned sources: S1 = Cybersecurity Best Practices for the Safety of Modern Vehicles, Updated 2022 final (87 FR 55459; docket NHTSA-2020-0087); S2 = Automated Driving Systems 2.0: A Vision for Safety (DOT HS 812 442, September 2017); S3 = 49 CFR Part 571 FMVSS selections (GovInfo annual edition 2024). → ch01
- **SAE levels of driving automation**: The taxonomy ADS 2.0 adopts by name (the SAE J3016 lineage, cited by designation only) rather than writing its own scale. The guidance targets Levels 3-5; level determination is the entity's responsibility against SAE's published definitions. → ch05, ch06
- **SBOM (software bill of materials)**: The software-component tracking habit behind [G.11]: sufficient detail that a newly disclosed open-source or off-the-shelf flaw maps quickly to affected ECUs and fielded vehicles. → ch02
- **Self-certification**: The US regulatory default: the manufacturer determines that the vehicle meets each applicable FMVSS and certifies accordingly at sale. There is no NHTSA pre-market type approval; enforcement runs through compliance testing, defects investigation, and recall. → ch01, ch07
- **SHSO (state highway safety official)**: The State official audience for ADS 2.0 Section 2's seven-block framework: administrative structure, test applications, permission, test drivers, registration and titling, public safety officials, and liability and insurance. → ch01, ch06
- **SOTIF (safety of the intended functionality)**: The concern named by ISO 21448: hazards that arise when the system is free of relevant faults but the intended function is still insufficient, such as perception or decision limits of an ADS. Complements fault-oriented functional safety; the standard is citation-only here. → ch08
- **TPMS (tire pressure monitoring system)**: The FMVSS 138-regulated system that warns drivers of significant under-inflation and of TPMS malfunction within stated detection windows and telltale rules. → ch07
- **Twelve priority safety design elements**: ADS 2.0 Section 1's consensus checklist, in source order: System Safety; Operational Design Domain; Object and Event Detection and Response; Fallback (Minimal Risk Condition); Validation Methods; Human Machine Interface; Vehicle Cybersecurity; Crashworthiness; Post-Crash ADS Behavior; Data Recording; Consumer Education and Training; and Federal, State, and Local Laws. → ch05
- **VSSA (Voluntary Safety Self-Assessment)**: The encouraged, public statement of how an entity addresses the twelve safety elements (or marks an element not applicable). Optional, not submitted for Federal approval, not a deployment gate, and free of CBI if published. → ch05
